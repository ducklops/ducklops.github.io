
## Intro

I’ve had my eye on Timberborn for a *long* time so when the devs came forward to tell me about the new update, I got stuck in right away. For the last week or so, I’ve been elbow-deep in one of the biggest updates this game has ever had and I’d love to tell you all about it. There’s new maps, new ways to build, new snazzy outfits, and a lot more. Let’s start with what got *me* the most excited first.

## Statistics Tracking

You know me – I love me a bit of statistics tracking.The new update brings these brand new statistics pages to track goods and population over time, using a nice customisable graph tool. The new page is extremely in-depth. It could be criticised for being slightly confusing, but once you get past the learning curve it’s a very handy tool to have in your back pocket. You can sort between Global and per-district views too. This is an extremely useful option to have to help narrow down your problems across districts instead of having to do the mental maths. You can see exactly where you districts are struggling and adjust your development accordingly — there’s no point building houses to solve a homelessness crisis in the wrong district.

## New Maps

That’s not all the update brought. Two new maps have been added to the game.
Pillars has been added - it’s a new map with fractured terrain. Instead of one sprawling land mass, you build across these broken, disconnected- well, pillars. It’s a real challenge to manage, with a ‘floor is lava’ vibe with the huge badwater floor. It’s sure to be a difficult one to get around, and I could see some really interesting segmented bases being built. I’m sure it would help you keep organised; you could keep one industry on each section, so I’m looking forward to seeing more creative ways to utilise this map. Tag me in any posts you see!
The other new map is Nomads. Now that’s a cool name. In this map, the water flows gradually change over time. This could be *really* challenging. The river keeping your district alive and powered could just move by the time the next cycle comes around. This means that you’ll need to have multiple redundancies and I’m sure this map is where the pumps are going to come in handy. This will likely be a huge strain on your resources, so an enormous challenge even for seasoned players.

## Watertight Building

In case you didn’t realise, the entire shtick that Timberborn has is beavers, who like to disrupt the rivers across the map. So we’ve got even *more* ways to leverage this. Airlocks have arrived. It took me a good minute to figure out what they were for, but it’s completely obvious now that I look at it. Airlocks can seal off your entry points that lead to areas liable to flooding. Perfect if you still need access, but don’t want the water to spread to the rest of the colony, or it’s protecting something like a forest from badwater. Impermeable power shafts have also arrived. They hold back the water, while carrying power within them. This can be really helpful to use them as powered levees. It saves space while holding back the water. Compact mechanical pumps are here too. They’re here for when you don’t have enough space for a big mechanical pump, but you need to move some fluid between two reservoirs. They require a bit more power than the big guys, but if you can easily afford the 4k research points it takes to unlock it, I’m sure your network has plenty of power capacity. All of these watertight updates really fit into the maps we spoke about earlier. With Nomad’s shifting waters, you’re really going to need all of these tools to manage the flows as much as possible. This was really intentional, and it’s great that they’ve thought so holistically about the game in this way.

## Well-Being Attractions

There’s a bunch of different new well-being attractions for both Folktails and Iron Teeth. I’ve spent a lot of time in Folktails, but less so in Iron Teeth so on-screen you can see the Folktails additions: the sauna and the domed garden. Iron Teeth players got a massager and a dance pit to help to wind down after a hard day’s industrialism. The split between the really chill and relaxing additions compared to a mosh pit is a really funny way of trying to show the distinction the two factions should have.

## Succulents

I love a good cactus, which is why I’m so happy that succulents have been added to the game. They only grow in the desert areas, and can even be overwatered if they are placed underwater or in irrigated land for too long.  They actually store water, which can be harvested to stop populations from going thirsty during long droughts or if they’re situated in particularly dry environments. A growing emergency water supply is absolutely perfect for, I don’t know… the new map, Pillars? They thought of everything, folks. But, it’s not only useful on dry maps. If you’ve dammed too many sources on a regular map it’s entirely possible that you might have water security issues, or even it could be helpful at the start of a game before you’ve gotten the infrastructure to cope with droughts or badtides.

## Outfits

You didn’t think they’d leave the beavers out did you? They’ve all got some snazzy new outfits. There are separate styles for scavengers, miners, foresters, gatherers, and lumberjacks. They’re purely cosmetic, but it’s really nice to break up the population visually. Instead of all of them being boring beavers, there’s new ways to tell them apart. Kind of like the detailers too.

## T4 Monuments

What’s more is a new monument has been added to the 4th tier for both Folktails and Iron Teeth. For Folktails, the glorious Hall of Abundance was added. Iron Teeth got the Arch of Progress. These are two incredibly grandiose buildings, that sit right between the 3rd tier buildings and the final stretch for a Wonder and winning the map. This fills a bit of a gap in the progression, to add a stepping stone before you just have to go for that endgame building.
