## Intro

Two absolute *classics* in the zoo management genre, with both clear differences and genuine similarities. Just months separate their releases, but is there a clear winner between the two? Let’s find out!

## [1] Animals

Jumping straight in with the heart of a zoo management, which game has better animals? Well, let’s start with Zoo Tycoon 2. Zoo Tycoon 2’s animals are, well, plentiful? Let’s start with that. Zoo Tycoon 2 has over 130 animals from the various DLCs. They have a huge amount of extinct species, dinosaurs, marine animals, living species, and heck, even a killer penguin just for the giggles. Zoo Tycoon 2’s variety of animals is what gives it such a strong appeal. The huge amount of DLCs means you can expand your collection in your parks hugely, and even the base game has a pretty solid selection of animals for you to play with. The models also aren’t half bad, and we’ll be saving the animal behaviours for later on in the video. The models were designed with realism in mind, and, for 2004, they weren’t too far off. In Jurassic Park: Operation Genesis, while they do only have dinosaurs, they have quite a few of them: 25 in total. While this may seem measly compared to JWE3 numbers, for 2003, the models are really detailed, and maintain their clarity when you get up close to them, and even as they move around their environments. Unfortunately, it would be stupid not to give this category to Zoo Tycoon 2. I wonder if it can still hold up so well in terms of animal behaviours. But first, building!

## [2] Building

Okay, Zoo Tycoon 2 has a pretty subtle grid-based tool. You get quite a large amount of control over where you place each of your items, and you can add quite a substantial amount of decoration across your park. You get a *huge* amount of items to play with, from creepy crawly houses to aquariums, and the tool is really in-depth. Also, there are some restrictions on where you can place items, called Conservation Areas, which add that extra level of challenge to your park as you need to connect your park using the various transportation items. Over in JP:OG, you, again, get a grid to place your items on. Path placing *is* a pain, but don’t even get me started on enclosure fencing. For some reason unknown to man, the fences place themselves in weird patterns, preventing you from precisely designing the shape of your enclosures, and leaving you with huge chunky boxes. This means there are enormous gaps left between your fences and enclosures. When you place down buildings, you get this nice little icon showing you where the building access points will be. This can be really nice and helpful when trying to build the amenities and facilities for your park, as the grid system can make it a bit of a pain to make the placement actually look nice. All the buildings are pretty nicely themed to Jurassic Park, but with such poor quality models, I’m not sure if this is hugely positive. Overall, I don’t particularly like building in either game, but Zoo Tycoon 2 has that little bit extra that gives it an edge over JP:OG, allowing it to take this category.

## [3] Behaviour

Right, the animal behaviours. In JP:OG the dinos will practice social behaviours such as defensively butting heads, and their movements are relatively realistic. They can break out and attack your guests, and are deterred by things like LITERAL SENTRY TURRETS. Not me, though. The dinosaurs have a nice selection of little animations and things they can do, such as drinking from lakes or going for a little wander, and it’s really lovely. They can even be carried away by dino-nadoes. Now that’s what I call ultra-realism. In Zoo Tycoon 2, there are a bunch of cool behaviours that you can’t find in many other games. They feature the controversial marine shows, where performing animals put on shows for crowds of guests in specially made show tanks, which are really cool as you can actually customise the show sequence yourself, making things really unique. What’s more, they have a bunch of different behaviours they can perform in their enclosures, like when predator-class animals attack prey, if they are matched together. Also, they can interact with different items from around their enclosures, making the game just feel more alive. Both of these games have really suitable behaviour systems for their animals. Zoo Tycoon 2 has a bunch of unique ones, while JP:OG allows for a lot of interaction between animals. This has been a difficult one for me to decide, but I’m gonna have to give one point to each game here.

## [4] Management

Management! My personal favourite category because without a good management system, what’s the point in playing a game at all. Let’s start off with Jurassic Park: Operation Genesis. In JP:OG, I’m just gonna come out and say it. The management system is somewhere on the line between hippopotamus poo, and not half bad. Firstly, you get all of your stats and hats, but not hats in the Reports section, all broken down into individual menus for ultra-efficiency. Once you get into these reports, they are broken down really nicely, and you can usually cycle between a couple of different menus packed with information about your park. This can actually come in really handy when you’re trying to optimise your park, and figure out your weak spots. The actions button takes you to all of your critical actions such as performing emergency ticket price increases because your pocket is wearing thin, or declaring a literal emergency because it’s getting Jurassic World 1 up in here. Side-note: you can’t convince me that any reasonable governing body would allow a business to reopen after over 200 people were brutally dismembered by dinosaurs that shouldn’t even exist at this point. Anyway, you can also manage everything to do with your dinosaurs through the Dinosaur menu. Here, you can manage your fossil expeditions, to a *maximum* of five dig sites. Your extracting of stuff can be sped up through research, and here, you can also unlock things such as turrets, shelters, and other completely insanely over-the-top features. Okay, Zoo Tycoon 2-o’clock. You can see a really deep look at your finances, really emphasising that overwhelming debt. In Zoo Tycoon 2, you get a whole lot less control over the management aspect, but there are other features such as that cool little fossil hunt minigame, or the ability to use a really nice map to have a look at your whole park layout in one go. Anyway, Jurassic Park: Operation Genesis just pulls ahead here of Zoo Tycoon 2, but it was a close one.

## [5] Terrain

“T” is for terrain, or toothbrushes. I’m sorry I thought that would be funny. Zoo Tycoon 2 has a genuinely not abysmal terrain system for its time. You can lift terrain, sink it down, smoothen it out, and it all works with kind of a grid system. The tool is really smooth, and to be fair, quite well made. In JP:OG, though, you get to play around with the Environment tool. Trees and paleo trees can be found separately, but when it comes to the terrain itself, you can’t precisely raise or lower terrain like normal, you have to use the mountain and flatten tool which is all a lot more extreme than it should be. Water is a massive pain, you build lakes with no ability to change the radius of the tool, resulting in entire oceans being placed all at once. Moving back to Zoo Tycoon 2 just to really throw you off, the water tool is so detailed. To the extent that it might actually be a little bit *too* much. You get to pick from a bunch of different depths that affect the outside terrain too, based on how far away from the coast you go, and you also have to pick relevant biomes for the animals in the enclosures, otherwise they will be really sad. With JP:OG’s simplicity, and Zoo Tycoon 2’s [] elaborateness, it’s going to be difficult to make a decision. I think Zoo Tycoon 2 needs to take this category, as it gives the player so much more control than JP:OG.

## Conclusion

So, who won? Well, JP:OG took 2 categories, while Zoo Tycoon 2 won 4, meaning Zoo Tycoon 2 won!
