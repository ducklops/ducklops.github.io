## Introduction

How does a classic in the Jurassic Park series hold up against Jurassic World Evolution 2? Does newer mean better, or are the classics still holding up well? Let’s find out!

## [1] Animals

Well, in Jurassic Park: Operation Genesis, the models are certainly simple, but surprisingly detailed for their time. The closer you get to them with your camera, the easier it is to see: they don’t look terrible. Also, the dinosaurs’ behaviours are nothing short of awesome for their time. The animals have various animations and ‘things’ they can do, interacting with their environment by drinking from lakes, and interacting with each other to practice defensively butting heads, and more. If it wasn’t for dino-nadoes, so far, JP:OG would be absolutely smashing this category. But there are dino-nadoes. In Jurassic World Evolution 2, the dinosaur behaviours are even better. Entire dinosaur social groups perform actions together, and, in true Jurassic World fashion, are *obsessed* with destroying my rangers cars. The models are even more detailed than JP:OG, and you can customise and change their skin textures and colours through research, leading to even more immersion in JWE2. The animals in the games are pretty similar and relate to those in the Jurassic Universe, so there’s not much you can complain about. Obviously, they aren’t realistic, but they *are* great. It’s close, but the more detail in JWE2’s models, and the improved behaviours allow it to take this category.

## [2] Building

In Jurassic Park: Operation Genesis, building enclosures is, how do you say it? Oh, *awful*. For a game released in 2003, they certainly were ahead of their time with the implementation of social distancing between existing objects and fences. You *can* get around this by placing your paths after the fences, but just let me do it how I want! Placing down buildings is actually pretty nice. They work basically on a grid system, and you get this nice little icon of where the access points for each building you place are gonna be. In Jurassic World Evolution 2, building is not bound to a grid, but that doesn’t necessarily mean it’s any better at all. In fact, building is still awkward and paths so frequently get in the way that it really impacts and inconveniences gameplay. I wish there was a way to disable collisions between buildings and paths or just allow objects to be placed closer together because it really is a pain. Building enclosures in JWE2 is actually pretty nice, though. You get a more Planet Zoo-esque fence building tool, where you pick the type of fence you’re placing, and get a lot of freedom in how and where they are placed, making enclosure building in JWE2 so much easier. In JP:OG, you end up with really horrible boxy shaped enclosures and it’s just ugly. Clearly, both games have one aspect where they are stronger, and one where they are weaker. Hmmm… I give up. I have to give one point to each game here.

## [3] Management

There’s not a lot to manage in JP:OG, but you can unlock dig sites to dig for different dinosaur fossils, but, for some reason, you can only unlock 5 of them. I suppose they must have some pretty strong anti-monopoly laws in the Jurassic World Universe. To speed up the process of getting fossils in JP:OG, you can just buy any fossils available. Apparently there’s quite a substantial market for pre-existing ankylosaurus fossils. Otherwise, you can send out dig teams to go dig up fossils with their teams. The more fossils you have and extract of a specific dinosaur, the longer they will live in your park, so to avoid the costs of the dino-undertaker, and the trauma you will induce on anyone visiting your park, it’s best to do this. In JP:OG, I am obsessed with the map generation. It looks like this style of generating a map is coming back in JWE3, but you can adjust the trees, rivers, mountains, shape of the island, all to give you a really deep level of control when building your new park. Enough glaze, though, in JP:OG, it is wildly boring waiting for money to come in. That’s why my ticket prices are so extortionate, but we’ll come back to this later on. Other key aspects of management like your helicopters can be extremely janky when playing on a modern system due to compatibility with the new OS versions, but you can’t really blame JP:OG for this. The reports section of the JP:OG management is really mediocre. It’s frankly useless, but I do like how there’s a bit of personality in your quarterly reports, for example. Anyway, returning viewers will know how much I love the JWE2 management system, but, if you’re new or want a recap, it’s like a central computer system for managing your entire park. You get a super cool map to see where all of your teams, dinosaurs, and facilities are; your scientists can lash out and sabotage your park if you don’t treat them well enough; you have access to the Jurassic World Database; and you can see your missions, finances, park rating, all in such deep detail - it’s really great. You can manage your expeditions to a huge amount of dig sites, and, get this: you can even go to more than 5 of them! I mean, how crazy is that? You could go to like 7 dig sites! What?! There’s also a huge amount of research to unlock that could justify its own category. Overall, it would be wrong not to give this category to Jurassic World Evolution 2.

## [4] Staff

Staff, staff, staff - it doesn’t even sound like a real word to me anymore with all these games I’ve compared. JWE2’s staff system is not half bad. You have to hire scientists, and build response facilities, paleo-medical centres, and expedition centres, housing teams for your park handling different tasks from dinosaur healing to the obtaining of fossils. Your capture teams also are in charge of making sure none of your dinosaurs step out of line. Personally, I love hopping in the helicopters, tranquillising my dinosaurs, or driving the ranger cars directly into my guests. Unfortunately, you are always unable to actually run them over. Your scientists can become disgruntled and will sabotage your park - as you do - so you need to manage their rest times in between their sciencey tasks. As well as cars and choppers, your staff also need to supervise your avian and marine dinosaurs. To do this, they control aerial and underwater drones to perform checks, and do everything that would normally be done from the sky or ground, such as administering medicine, or tranquillising for movement. In JP:OG, you would have also been able to take control of your vehicles. Unfortunately, due to how incredibly old this game is, I couldn’t get that aspect working properly. You need to build stations for cleaners, ranger stations, and you can research literal sentry turrets to defend against your dinosaurs. Where is this in JWE2? Maybe JWE3. You never know. Jurassic Park: Operation Genesis, while it doesn’t have such deep control over scientists, it has a much more well-rounded approach to staff. JWE2 is rather lackluster in your other aspects of staff, so JP:OG will take this category.

## [5] Terrain

This seems to be a recurring theme that hopefully will be fixed with the release of JWE3, but both games have really bad terrain systems, so the aim of this category is going to have to be to decide the least painful to use. So, in Jurassic World Evolution 2, you have a smooth raise and lower terrain tool, as well as a flatten and roughen tool. There are really strict limits to how much you can manipulate terrain, and other than the inclusion of contour lines, it’s terrible. There is a forest brush tool to help you more easily populate the enclosures with shrubbery and other vegetation, and, if I wasn’t bothered about how the rock design in my enclosures looked, I’d say the tool was pretty okay. It will by default randomly switch between different rock types and rotations, but, like I said in chapter 2 talking about the building tools, it’s really tough to place them close together, really harming your ability to creatively express. Also, there is one preset depth of water - it is either there or it isn’t. In Operation Genesis, the situation is even worse. You can build, in sets of 9, paleo and modern trees. You can flatten the land, build a lake, and as well as that, you can build mountains with a preset shape and size. At least you can change the brush radius and mountain height in Jurassic World Evolution 2. I suppose that’s where an extra 18 years of industry development gets you. JWE2 takes this category, without a doubt.

## [6] Guests

First, let’s talk about the guests in Operation Genesis. They are, well, terrifying. They’re worse than Prehistoric Kingdom. I get that these models are from 2003 or something, but why are they all so petrifying? Enough of my whining, having to save guests from the dinosaurs is pretty cool, and they each have really simple requirements to keep them happy. One funny thing about the guests is that they will literally pay whatever you ask them to, to get into your park. By default, the fare was down to like $200, but I have these little idiots paying a grand just to get through the gates. You don’t even want to know how much I’m charging for toilet use. It’s strange how suddenly nobody needs to use the toilets when you charge $10 a poo. The guests arrive every now and then via helicopter, and the system is super basic. Anyway, in Jurassic World Evolution 2, your guests are categorised into 4 different categories of guests: General, Adventure, Nature, and Luxury guests. Each different type of guest appreciates different things more, from infamous dinosaurs for the adventurous folks, to getting up close to the dinosaurs enclosures for those more eco-conscious. The guests need different types of highly customisable amenities, that can be gradually unlocked via research. They can also sue you, and protest, so that’s fun. Jurassic World Evolution 2 has a significantly *better* guests system that JP:OG. It would be wrong for it not to win this category.
